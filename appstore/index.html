Mac App Store edition

Same clipboard, one extra keystroke.

You're here because you got clip and cue from the Mac App Store. This page covers how that edition works and the one place it differs from the version on this site.

The short version

Copy with ⌘C like you always do. clip and cue remembers everything — text, images, files, design objects.
Click the paperclip in the menu bar, or press ⌘⌥V for the numbered pop-up, and pick a clip. Your cursor stays right where it was.
Press ⌘V. That's it. The clip lands exactly as you copied it — a Keynote shape comes back an editable shape, not a screenshot of one.

Why the extra ⌘V?

The direct-download edition can paste for you automatically, which uses a macOS permission the Mac App Store doesn't allow apps to use for that purpose. So the App Store edition keeps things simple: picking a clip loads your clipboard, and you paste it yourself.

The upside: this edition never asks for any system permission. No System Settings visits, no toggles, nothing to grant — and nothing to break when the app updates. Install it and start copying.

What's identical in both editions

  • Full-fidelity capture — the complete clipboard, every format, editable round-trip back into Keynote, Illustrator, Word and friends
  • The quick-paste pop-up with number keys
  • Pins, stacks, multi-file rows with previews
  • Password-manager copies ignored by default
  • Everything stays on your Mac; optional iCloud sync through your own private database

One small difference with stacks

Pasting a whole stack in one go relies on the same automatic-paste machinery, so in this edition you fan a stack open with the chevron and grab its pieces one at a time. Multi-file rows still load all their files in a single go.

Updates

The App Store handles them. Nothing to check, nothing to download from here.
